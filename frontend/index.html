Project Overview

Acoustic Feature Extraction & Multi-Layer Perceptron Classification for Affective Computing

Core Title

Speech Emotion Recognition from Acoustic Features using a Multi-Layer Perceptron Classifier

A baseline affective computing study quantifying the discriminative capability of MFCC, Chroma, and Mel-spectrogram descriptors on acted vocal emotion.

📋

Problem Statement

Human speech conveys nuanced affective cues through acoustic modulations in pitch, vocal timbre, and spectral energy. In customer helplines and telehealth counselling, the inability to automatically detect vocal emotional distress leads to delayed escalation and unrecognised mental health crises. Frontline workers and counsellors currently rely entirely on subjective judgment. This prototype investigates whether raw acoustic descriptors can directly categorise discrete emotional states without subjective listener review.

🎯

Research Question

"Can acoustic features extracted from a raw speech signal — specifically Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients (MFCC), Chroma, and Mel-Spectrogram energy — be used to reliably classify a speaker's discrete emotional state (calm, happy, fearful, disgust) using a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron classifier, and how does the resulting baseline accuracy compare against the more complex deep-learning approaches reported in the literature?"

🔬

Hypothesis (Falsifiable)

We hypothesise that MFCC, Chroma, and Mel-spectrogram features contain sufficient discriminative information for a lightweight MLP classifier to distinguish between calm, happy, fearful, and disgust at an accuracy meaningfully above the 25% random-guess baseline.

Falsifiability Criterion: Acoustically similar pairs (fearful vs. disgust, both characterised by tense, lower-energy vocalisation) will exhibit a higher mutual misclassification rate than acoustically distinct pairs (happy vs. fearful). If confusion errors are distributed uniformly across all class pairs, the hypothesis is falsified.
🌐

SDG 3 Mapping & Relevance

SDG 3 Good Health and Well-Being • Target 3.4 (Mental Health)

Target 3.4 prioritises the promotion of mental health and well-being. In remote counselling and tele-triage platforms, an automated SER baseline can flag acoustic distress signals (such as acute fear or panic) in real time, alerting counsellors to prioritise urgent interventions. Minimising false negatives on the fearful class directly addresses this health priority.

Dataset & Preprocessing

RAVDESS Benchmark Speech Corpus and 4-Emotion Working Subset

RAVDESS Benchmark Overview

The Ryerson Audio-Visual Database of Emotional Speech and Song (RAVDESS) is an internationally recognized benchmark released under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 academic licence. It features 24 professional North American actors (12 male, 12 female) vocalising two lexically matched sentences across 8 emotions at two emotional intensities.

4-Class Experimental Subset

To establish a computationally tractable yet genuinely multi-class baseline, the label set is filtered to 4 discrete emotions, yielding exactly 768 audio files with perfect 1:1:1:1 balance:

02 Calm 192 files
03 Happy 192 files

Filename format: [modality]-[channel]-[emotion]-[intensity]-[statement]-[repeat]-[actor].wav

Data Dictionary

Feature Name Data Type Dimensionality Acoustic & Clinical Description
MFCC (mean) float[40] 40 coefficients Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients averaged over time frames; captures short-term spectral envelope (vocal timbre).
Chroma STFT (mean) float[12] 12 pitch bins Pitch-class energy distribution from Short-Time Fourier Transform; captures harmonic/pitch content.
Mel Spectrogram (mean) float[128] 128 frequency bands Frequency-domain energy representation on the mel scale, approximating human auditory perception.
Sample Rate integer Scalar (Hz) Native sampling rate of each .wav file; used internally during librosa feature extraction.
Emotion Label categorical 1 target class Ground-truth emotion parsed from 3rd field in 7-part filename: 02=calm, 03=happy, 06=fearful, 07=disgust.
Actor ID categorical Identifier (01-24) Speaker ID from 7th field; tracks gender and actor balance across train/test splits.

Documented Preprocessing Steps

1

Missing Value Handling

All 768 files in the 4-class subset verified to load without I/O errors; zero missing or corrupted audio samples.

2

Categorical Encoding

Parsed directly from standardized filename identifiers; scikit-learn's MLPClassifier performs internal target mapping.

3

Feature Scaling

Not applied in this baseline. MFCC, Chroma, and Mel features used raw as a documented baseline limitation.

4

Outlier Treatment

No outlier removal performed on extracted vectors to preserve raw acoustic variance for baseline characterisation.

5

Train / Test Split

75:25 holdout split using train_test_split(..., test_size=0.25, random_state=9) (576 train, 192 test).
